## Gameplay pipeline

1. `StartMenuController` selects a difficulty and opens the first stage, or resumes the second stage when its story progress marker is present. `GameProgress` coordinates clearing a run. `StoryProgress` and `Difficulty` use `PlayerPrefs`; `RunProgress`, `RunInventory`, and `RunEquipment` hold the current play session's abilities, items, and gear.
2. Each stage instantiates the Hero and UI. `Role` reads the Input System keyboard and drives movement, jump, dash, melee, and kunai states. `PlayerProgression` applies equipped and forged attack/defense values. `UIManager` coordinates the bag, forge, minimap, and pause panels.
3. Flying Eyes, Mushrooms and Skeletons use Orc-style `EntityState` objects and a Visual Animator (see the pipelines below); Goblins retain the legacy `MobStateMachine`. Each arena Boss uses `EnemyPlatformNavigator` for pursuit and `BossTeleport` for its authored reposition window. The navigator derives transient landing spots from arena colliders, checks jump arcs against walls, and searches the resulting graph with A*. `BossArenaController` supplies arena bounds and changes cameras, music, minimap visibility, and story presentation when the Hero enters.
4. `EnemyAttackController` chooses an eligible `EnemyAttackPattern` by range and weight, owns its coroutine and cooldown, and sends cast callbacks to `BossStateMachine`. The pattern owns telegraph, hitbox, projectile, damage, and impact feedback. Combat and hit timing do not depend on animation events.
5. `BossStateMachine` sends Idle/Run movement changes to the visual child's Unity Animator Controller. At cast start it selects Attack1/2/3; during windup it samples the clip through its authored release frame using the pattern's charge progress; the pattern's fire callback starts the follow-through. Damage outside a cast plays Hurt; death plays Death. The King and Wizard have separate Controllers and sprite clips under `Assets/Animations/Boss`. The Wizard's Attack3 state intentionally reuses its Attack1 frames. The visual `SpriteRenderer` flips independently of the Boss collider; the King retains its off-centre-pivot correction.
6. `EnemyHealth` handles Boss defeat: the first Boss preserves run inventory and moves to the next stage; final victory clears progress and returns to the menu. `StoryDialogueController` plays scene-authored dialogue, comics, and one-shot tutorials.

## Hero Role pipeline

The Hero root in `Assets/Prefab/Hero.prefab` has two script components: `Role` for player behavior and `Entity_VFX` for the shared hit flash. Rigidbody2D, CapsuleCollider2D and AudioSource remain Unity components. The visual child retains Animator, SpriteRenderer and the shared `Entity_AniamtionTriggers` event relay. `Entity_Combat` belongs to enemies; the former HeroHealth, HeroKunaiThrow, HeroAttackAudio and PlayerDropThrough components and source files have been removed.

1. `Role.Awake` builds the existing movement/combo/throw states, initializes its health pool from `maximumHealth`, configures combo audio, and connects the scene-authored HP bar and defeat overlay. The four saved gameplay scenes reference Role directly; their prefab parameter overrides have been moved onto the surviving Role component. This migration preserves scene tuning: stage one has 200 HP and stage two currently has 20000 HP, with a 3.5 melee radius in both campaign stages.
2. Input System keys select movement, jump, dash, melee and throw states. Combo entry calls `Role.PlayAttackSound`. The model's `AttackTrigger` event routes to `Role.Attack`, which checks the authored target point/radius, filters opposing living `IDamageable` actors, deduplicates multiple colliders on one actor, and applies the current melee damage once to each target. Enemy animation events still route to their own Entity_Combat.
3. The throw state checks `Role.CanThrowKunai` before entering and retains its existing gravity suspension and cooldown. At the authored release frame (0.25 seconds), `ThrowTrigger` calls `Role.FireKunai`: validate the projectile, consume exactly one RunInventory kunai, spawn it at the muzzle/root, and launch horizontally along `facingside`. FlyingEyeProjectile2D performs collision and opposing-faction damage through IDamageable. Speed 60 and damage 30 remain independently authored kunai values; weapon forging continues to affect melee only. Empty inventory, disabled control and defeat prevent release.
4. `PlayerProgression` remains the scene's bridge to shared run data and UI. Role's Inspector `Base Attack` (serialized as `damage`) is the persistent base. `Role.AttackPower` calculates **base attack + equipped weapon attackBonus (zero when unarmed) + weapon forge level × 10** directly from current run data. `Role.Damage` applies any separate damage multiplier after that sum. Startup, equipment changes and forging never write the result back into the base or reset the multiplier, so bonuses cannot accumulate across refreshes. `PlayerProgression.WeaponAttack` and the forge's weapon before/after preview read the same Role calculation; bag item details show only the weapon's own attack plus its forge contribution. For example, 23 base + 10 weapon + level-two forging (20) gives 53 attack, and the next forge preview is 63. Armor retains its existing equipment/forge calculation and calls `Role.SetDefense`. Spikes, enemy attacks and projectiles call `Role.ApplyDamage` through Role or IDamageable. Role ignores invalid/dead/match-over damage, subtracts defense with a minimum of one damage, updates the HP bar and HealthChanged event, flashes the model, and applies the existing knockback.
5. A worn Green Rune regenerates the same health pool in Role.Update at `2 + 2 * forgeLevel` HP per scaled second. Health Potion use now resolves Role and calls RestoreFullHealth; partial RestoreHealth clamps to maximum and cannot revive a defeated Hero. Item pickups, portals, chests and story/arena triggers also recognize Role or the faction-aware IDamageable contract.
6. At zero health, Role marks the match over, disables control and physics, cancels a queued combo, greys the sprites, shows the defeat overlay and emits Defeated. Role continues checking R while dead so the current stage can reload. Existing RunInventory, RunEquipment and forge progress carry through that reload.
7. S or Down uses Role's Input System input to raycast the one-way platform under the feet and temporarily ignore only that platform's collider. Collision is restored after the authored delay or when Role is disabled. Role and enemy CombatHealth register in `CombatTargets` while enabled so enemy AI can acquire the Hero without requiring a separate health component; a cutscene control pause leaves the actor registered.

Run `Tools/Hero/Validate Role Components` to check the prefab, both campaign stages and both legacy Hero scenes. `HeroRolePlayModeTests` exercises component ownership/target acquisition, armor and forge values, health/HUD/regeneration, melee animation events, actual throw input and release timing, ammunition exhaustion, defeat/restart inventory, and platform collision restoration. The inventory item-detail and forge display tests cover the existing UI integrations.

## Boss movement and collision pipeline

- `BossArenaController.ArenaMin/Max` bounds the search to the active Boss room. `BossLandingGraph` scans the ground collision layer in short vertical segments so lower ledges on a `TilemapCollider2D` are found as well as top surfaces. It adds candidates near collider centres and edges for narrow spawned platforms, rejects side walls, triggers, spots without full Boss-body clearance, and spots outside the arena. Sampling currently caps the graph at 256 spots per arena; the tested campaign arenas produce fewer than 100. These samples exist only in memory; the two campaign scenes contain no `EnemyNavigationNode` objects.
- The graph considers a directed jump only within the authored horizontal, vertical, and total distance limits, then checks the whole parabolic arc using the Boss collider footprint. A* chooses a route to the landing surface nearest the Hero. The King selects the first step of a reachable route away from the Hero for its retreat hop; the Wizard chooses a safe distant sampled surface immediately before its blink. The attack controller still owns relocation frequency and timing.
- Every 0.5 seconds the navigator compares arena colliders and their bounds. A new, removed, enabled, disabled, or moved collider invalidates sampled spots and the route. A platform spawner can call `EnemyPlatformNavigator.NotifyGeometryChanged(changedBounds)` for an immediate next-physics-step refresh. Dynamic collision geometry must be on the Boss ground layer; continuously moving platforms and in-place Tilemap edits are outside this version's navigation contract. The current hop also checks each movement segment; an obstruction cancels it and lets gravity settle the Boss before replanning.
- Outside a hop, the Boss keeps a dynamic `Rigidbody2D` with gravity and vertical collision response, but freezes horizontal physics motion. Player contact and residual velocity cannot slide it along a platform. During a pursuit or authored retreat hop, the navigator releases horizontal motion, temporarily disables gravity, and advances the existing parabolic route through `Rigidbody2D.MovePosition`.
- The hop endpoint restores gravity, clears horizontal velocity, and freezes horizontal motion while the collider settles onto a platform. Pursuit waits for actual ground contact before another jump, including when a landing platform disappears. The authored landing timeout releases an explicit relocation coroutine so attacks cannot wait forever; it does not allow pursuit to jump in mid-air. An attack interruption follows the same landing path; an explicit retreat hop continues during its owning attack. `EnemyHealth` disables Boss physics on defeat. Movement changes do not alter damage, attack release timing, jump speed, or jump height.
- To verify, run `BossRelocationPlayModeTests` for generated/removed surfaces, walls, retreat, contact stability, and both formal arena routes; run `Stage2KingFallsWithGravityAndCastCleanupKeepsThePhysicsPosition` and `BothCampaignBossesUseAnimatorAndKeepCastTiming` for existing combat behavior. `Tools/Boss/Remove Authored Navigation Nodes From Campaign` removes stale markers if old scenes are reimported. Play both arenas to inspect jump landings and wall approaches.

## Flying Eye state and animation pipeline

Stage one and stage two share `Assets/Enemy/Mobs/FlyingEye/Mob_FlyingEye.prefab`. The root owns `FlyingEyeController : Entity`, the shared `StateMachine`, six `EntityState` subclasses, `FlyingEyeRangedAttack`, `Enemy_Health`, Rigidbody2D, collider and hit flash. The existing `Visual` child owns SpriteRenderer, Animator and `Entity_AniamtionTriggers`. Its `FlyingEye.controller` graph has conditional Entry branches in **Dead ? Hurt ? Attack ? Chase ? Patrol ? Idle** priority, default Idle, and zero-duration exits when a state's bool becomes false. Dead has no outgoing transition. The code only sets parameters; no Animator.Play, CrossFade or runtime sprite-frame player switches the eye's animation.

1. **AI decision:** `FlyingEyeController.Update` finds the closest living Player through `CombatTargets` and updates the current state. Idle waits before Patrol; Patrol reverses within its spawn-centred bounds. An acquired target triggers Attack when in range and off cooldown, Chase beyond the preferred distance, or Idle during cooldown at close range. Losing a chased target returns to Idle. Its independent FixedUpdate preserves the authored patrol/chase speed and vertical tracking, without Entity's ground/wall probes. Only the Visual SpriteRenderer.flipX changes facing; the collider/root never rotates.
2. **State classes:** Idle, Patrol, Chase, Attack, Hurt and Dead each implement Enter/Update/Exit on the same foundation as Orc. Enter enables the state's Animator bool, Exit clears it, and Controller exits return to Entry to select the next bool. Attack/Hurt/Dead stop velocity. Animation clips bind only the Visual's SpriteRenderer.m_Sprite: Flight at 8 FPS for Idle, Flight at 12 FPS for Patrol/Chase, Attack1 at 12 FPS, Take Hit at 12 FPS, Death at 10 FPS.
3. **Attack preparation:** `FlyingEyeRangedAttack.BeginAttack` validates owner/target/range/cooldown, creates the existing warning disc/fill, then enters Attack. Animator plays the complete eight-frame clip; warning progress reads that state's normalized time. The float `AttackSpeed = 1 / Difficulty.MobWindupScale` accelerates the entire attack on Hard. Authored range, preferred distance, projectile speed, warning size, damage and cooldown remain separate serialized configuration; the obsolete eye `windupDuration` is removed.
4. **Release and recovery:** the seventh frame (index 6, clip time 0.5 seconds) emits `FlyingEyeShotRelease` through the Visual relay. The root only permits release while alive in Attack. Ranged attack deduplicates events, clears the warning and instantiates at most one `FlyingEyeProjectile2D`, aimed at the target's position **at release**. Normal releases near 0.5 seconds and finishes near 0.667; Hard releases near 0.3 and finishes near 0.4. Release retains Attack for the last frames. `FlyingEyeStateComplete("Attack")` marks the state finished; its next Update exits, cleans up and starts the normal cooldown. Difficulty continues to multiply damage and cooldown independently (Hard ?1.4 and ?0.65).
5. **Projectile and damage:** the projectile travels with the authored speed/lifetime, ignores its owner and allies, applies damage once to an opposing living IDamageable on collision, and then destroys itself. Terrain also consumes it. Already released projectiles remain independent of the owner's later Hurt/death/disable.
6. **Hurt:** nonlethal Enemy_Health damage notifies FlyingEyeController, which exits Attack if necessary, applies its normal cooldown, cancels pending release and removes the warning. Hurt stops flight and plays the complete four-frame clip. Further hits still deal damage and flash but do not re-enter or restart Hurt. `FlyingEyeStateComplete("Hurt")` resumes target decisions; a late event from an interrupted clip cannot finish another state.
7. **Death and reward:** lethal damage skips Hurt, marks health dead and calls EntityDeath. Dead cancels attack/warning, stops motion, disables Rigidbody2D simulation and the root collider, locks further state changes and plays the complete death clip. Enemy_Health awards its authored coinReward through PlayerProgression once; the health guard rejects later lethal hits. `FlyingEyeStateComplete("Dead")` removes the corpse. If a completion event is absent, the state exits/destroys after the current clip's duration (including attack speed) plus 0.1 seconds; this protection never releases a missing shot. Disable/destroy cancels warnings and pending attacks; enabling a living eye returns to Idle.
8. **Editor pipeline:** `Tools/A Thousand Battles Later/Migrate Flying Eye Animator` creates/updates the six clips, retains the prefab GUID and old state component ID, replaces that component's script, preserves the existing Visual/health bar/material/collider and movement/combat tuning, removes the old Visual MobSpriteAnimator, and wires Animator/relay/clips. Eye-specific stripped scene references and obsolete overrides are migrated without saving whole scenes. `EnemyContentBuilder` and `DemoSceneBuilder` route eye construction/configuration/validation through FlyingEyeAnimatorBuilder; the other species' implementation remains unchanged. `Validate Flying Eye Animator` inspects Controller parameters, Entry priority, exits, sprite-only bindings, clip events and saved references in campaign and legacy scenes.

`FlyingEyeAnimatorPlayModeTests` drives the actual Animator graph and checks normal/Hard release and recovery, single-shot deduplication, release-time aim, movement/facing, damage, interruptions, repeated Hurt, death priority/reward, disable/destroy cleanup, missing-event protection and every saved eye in both campaign scenes. `MobStateMachinePlayModeTests` distinguishes migrated eye/ground controllers from the legacy Goblin. Current authored eye counts are **7 in stage1_full and 8 in stage2_full**. Open an eye's Visual in the Animator window to inspect the live bool and Entry/Exit transitions. The broader legacy full-map tests still contain unrelated authored-content assumptions listed in TODO.

## Integrated wall/platform route pipeline (winding-r8)

1. Capture the initialized Hero's current equipment-adjusted traversal profile, choose dimensions and the unchanged size-based branch target. Retry retains the existing layout rather than recapturing movement.
2. Construct fixed-length route candidates and fit functional rectangular walls before candidate collapse. Landings distinguish ground, independent one-way platforms, solid wall tops and platforms touching a wall side. Main-route wall integration targets 50%, accepts 40–60%, excludes ground/spawn/exit, and must occur in all three thirds of route travel.
3. Wall-side climb modules reserve approach, wall-launch/return and exit space. Their specified contact face is validated explicitly. Wall-top modules replace support without an extra platform cap. Adjacent actions can share a wall; IDs, route ownership, served action indices and landing attachments are recorded in the signature.
4. Analytical body envelopes follow the profile's jump timing, dash timing, braking allowance and full collider size. Candidate walls are checked against the motion phases and landing headroom. These are pre-collapse geometry constraints, not runtime pathfinding or a post-generation repair pass. One-way upward passage remains distinct from an impassable solid wall.
5. Branches still select 2–4 outbound moves plus explicit return moves. Each candidate must fit at least one wall-top support; new walls are checked against both travel directions, the main route and other selected branches. Auxiliary routes use the same admission. The original branch-count policy and main-route length tolerance are retained.
6. Terrain WFC favours fewer detached decorative walls and reserves all functional walls. Encounters can use sufficiently wide wall tops, while climb, chest and branch protection stays intact. Tilemap generation omits platform art/colliders on wall tops and aligns side-platform endpoints with wall faces; all walls retain the castle palette and rectangular boundaries.
7. Stage tiles, collisions, enemies, chests and rewards before committing the map. Failure preserves old layout/progress and does not fall back to the disconnected r7 layout. Equipment, forging and reward-claim behaviour are unchanged.

## Size-driven exploration, loot and equipment pipeline (winding-r7)

1. **Capture at generation time.** New maps capture the initialized Hero's current effective speed, airborne speed, jump velocity, normal gravity, body size, dash and wall-jump parameters. Worn Crimson Rune modifiers participate: the same seed with different movement equipment can produce a different map. Temporary throw-hover gravity is excluded. Death/F6 retry rebuilds the existing layout and reward session directly, without solving again or writing old movement values back to the Hero. F5 captures live values; opening F1 refreshes editable movement fields.
2. **Size first, branches second.** The room remains 100–150 by 50–100 cells. `DungeonBranchPolicy` interpolates a monotone 3×3 capacity grid and rounds down, capped by usable route length, short-side depth and conservative branch area. The count depends on dimensions/profile/route multiplier, not a random roll. Seeds select placements. The panel reports count and limiting factors; signatures record policy version. Capacity calibration is exclusively a development tool.
3. **Sparse main route.** Main actions target 88–95% of their corresponding usable upward/horizontal budget, retain fractional landing surfaces and preserve entrance-to-exit distance × route multiplier. Horizontal crossings are distributed among rises instead of one long row, with seeded span variation. Most ordinary shelves use the minimum supported width; selected wider surfaces support route walls. Candidate selection favours fewer required landings. Required shelves are never deleted after generation.
4. **Round-trip exploration.** `DungeonBranchPlanner` admits 2–4 outbound moves and explicit return contracts before main-route selection. Each terminal has a five-cell chest landing. Admission checks body clearance, walls, descending one-way interception, protected areas and separated anchors. Joint selection must fit the requested count; failure preserves the old map. Optional unmarked side shelves reuse round-trip contracts and a shrinking budget (at most four). Branch/auxiliary reservations constrain terrain and enemy camps. Main and branch lengths are separate. No runtime finished-map reachability search or repair is performed.
5. **Reward snapshot.** `DungeonLootTable` stores five recipes. Unowned claymore, plate and crimson have first priority; green follows; remaining chests contain one potion, 100 coins and the authored kunai pickup quantity. Equal-priority equipment is seed-shuffled without replacement. Ownership includes bag and worn items. This version uses priority allocation; weighted selection remains future work. The session copies quantities and tracks every collected entry. Used supplies stay claimed; uncollected items remain available after retry. No ability orb is generated.
6. **UI and play.** `WfcDungeonGameplayBuilder` wires the reusable Alpha UI, one EventSystem and loot assets. F opens a nearby chest, B opens the bag, N opens forging. Gameplay panels and F1 are mutually exclusive and block gameplay/chest input. Existing slots, additive attack formula, forge chances/costs/limits and green regeneration are reused; crimson remains unforgeable. Chests and pickups belong to their map root. Replacement roots, doors and chests are prepared before commit, so failure cannot discard the current map or clear growth.
7. **Run lifecycle.** Retry and new maps retain inventory, coins, equipment and forge levels. Explicit New run clears these only after preparing a replacement, then restores movement unlocks. New maps use live equipment-modified movement; gear changes do not repave an existing map. Layout and reward signatures are separate. Developer tests exercise real Hero movement and loot/UI lifecycle independently of generation.

Current r7 evidence is recorded in `COMMIT_AUDIT.md`; the calibrated count curve and sample statistics are in [WFC_BRANCH_CALIBRATION.md](WFC_BRANCH_CALIBRATION.md); older results below do not certify r7. `WfcBranchCalibration.Run` covers nine sizes × 100 seeds with increasing branch counts; `ValidateIndependent` covers held-out odd sizes, rune profiles, route multipliers and enemy densities.

The top-right 4x4 area provides doorway/head clearance; the exit stands on the platform at row 15, with feet at cell `(18, 16)`. Eight platforms rise in two-cell increments, and neighbouring landing centres are at most three cells apart horizontally. Three isolated solid walls are selected from 2x2, 3x2 and 2x3 rectangles, with at least three empty cells separating each pair along a separating axis. Walls stay away from the outer border, the spawn, the doorway and the reserved jump corridors. No hazards or enemies are added to this first prototype.

### Generation pipeline

1. **Authored input:** `Assets/Development/WfcRoom/Settings.asset` holds room dimensions, module constraints, solver budgets, movement assumptions and the existing castle Tile/Sprite palette. `WfcRoomBuilder` creates this asset, copies the existing Grid prefab's art into Tiles with explicit collision semantics, wires the standalone Hero/HUD/camera/door, and saves the scene. Rebuild with `Tools/World/Build WFC 20x20 Test Room` after closing the test scene; save any untitled scene first. Existing palette asset GUIDs are retained. The builder's seed sweep is an offline development check.
2. **Platform domains:** `WfcRoomLayout` represents each height band as a WFC variable. Its domain contains candidate three-cell platforms; the final domain fixes a four-cell exit platform. Spawn clearance and the first jump prune the first domain. Adjacent-domain compatibility encodes the maximum horizontal separation, two-cell rise, conservative single-jump flight time and ceiling clearance. These are construction constraints, not a reachability test applied to a finished map.
3. **WFC solve:** `WfcConstraintSolver<T>` chooses the unresolved variable with the lowest weighted Shannon entropy, makes a seeded weighted observation, propagates compatibility constraints to neighbouring domains, and backtracks on contradictions. The version-1 generator uses `System.Random`; the same seed, settings and generator version reproduce the same module layout. Variables are **platform bands and rectangle slots**, not unconstrained individual pixel/tile cells: this is a module-level WFC/constraint-satisfaction implementation, with hand-authored compatibility rules rather than an overlapping-image model or an external package.
4. **Wall domains:** once platforms collapse, their jump trajectories define protected cells. The actor's full capsule size and a clearance margin are swept along each prescribed ballistic arc before any wall is selected. Wall candidates intersecting this space, endpoint clearances or platform cells never enter a domain. A second WFC pass assigns rectangular wall slots; pairwise constraints enforce separation. Internal contradictions use bounded backtracking/restarts (2048 observations per solve, up to 12 route attempts by default). Exhaustion reports failure and leaves the current room intact.
5. **Direct output:** a solved layout goes directly to `WfcRoomGenerator`. There is **no post-generation path search, reachability acceptance check, repair pass or unreachable-room rejection step**. Background, solid terrain and one-way platforms are written with batched `SetTilesBlock`; Tilemap and Composite collider geometry is rebuilt, then the spawn, door and Hero position are refreshed. Ground and platforms use layer 6; only platforms have `PlatformEffector2D`. The isolated room uses a camera showing the entire room. Selecting the generator in Play Mode draws the prescribed jump arcs as Gizmos.
6. **Player and exit:** the existing Hero `Role` retains input, movement, animation and collision behavior. This test instance has scale 5, one jump, no dash, jump velocity 64, gravity 147.15 and air speed 31.5; the generation profile matches its 2.2x5.496 capsule. The scene supplies its own correctly positioned ground probe and HUD references. Triggering `WfcRoomExit` updates the status only. Regeneration resets completion, refreshes colliders and returns the Hero to the fixed spawn.
7. **Development verification:** `WfcRoomBuilder.ValidateSeeds` checks structural constraints and determinism over seeds 0–199, and exercises backtracking on an impossible two-colour triangle. `WfcRoomPlayModeTests` checks generated colliders, the 3x3 clear spawn, seed controls, failure preservation, respawn, exit isolation, and real Hero input-driven jumps across complete routes. These tests verify the constraint design during development and are not part of runtime generation.

This prototype deliberately constrains the main route to an upward sequence; it does not yet generate branches, keys, ability gates, enemies or multiple connected rooms. Its movement contract assumes the stated Hero profile and baseline equipment. Changes to movement, actor size, tile scale or collider semantics require corresponding constraint updates and development tests. Future expansion should add compatible room/platform modules and ability-aware constraints, preserving direct generation instead of adding a post-generation reachability filter.

Verified with Unity **6000.5.2f1** on 2026-09-28: **4/4 WFC Play Mode tests passed**, including all eight consecutive single jumps for seeds **20260928, 17 and 199** using the real Hero and physics. The offline 200-seed sweep produced **200 distinct layouts**, each reproduced identically on a second solve; all succeeded on the first route attempt (400 solves plus structural checks took 436 ms on this machine). This measures the constraint solver, not total Tilemap/collider/rendering cost; the room HUD separately reports full generation time. The test keyboard is stepped explicitly; character transforms/velocities are not scripted during route traversal. The saved scene was also rendered and visually inspected. ## Retained multi-room WFC reference pipeline

The previous multi-room implementation is retained through `WfcDungeonLayout.GenerateMultiRoom` and its explicit regression tests. Its 120x80 default, sealed ability gates and white walls below describe this reference mode. **The saved WfcDungeon scene and builder now use the single winding room described in the next section.** The 20x20 scene remains a separate regression example.

### Generation and gameplay

1. **Effective Hero snapshot.** After `Role` and progression initialize, `TraversalProfile.Capture` reads actual ground/air speed, jump impulse, gravity, capsule size, maximum jumps, dash duration/speed/cooldown, wall-jump impulse/input lock and slide speed. Jump height is `v^2/(2g)`. Melee steering/lunge speed contributes to the conservative non-dash distance bound. Per the current scope, kunai hover is excluded from the movement contract; carrying kunai does not prevent generation.
2. **Required room structure.** Dimensions and the movement envelope establish minimum room size. An ordered main chain starts at the bottom-left room, places separate double-jump and dash challenges when unlocked, and ends at the upper-right wall-jump chamber and Boss-door artwork. Each additional room attaches once to a non-challenge room: this constructs a tree, so branches cannot reconnect past a required challenge. All shared portals have matching coordinates and body clearance. Low branches have rising return platforms.
3. **Action and surface constraints.** Double-jump uses a smooth step higher than one jump; dash uses a smooth-sided gap wider than the conservative non-dash flight budget, with recovery platforms only at its entrance. Their ceilings, perimeter walls and uninterrupted partitions exclude routes through neighbouring rooms. The wall-jump chamber uses two continuous climbable walls, a shaft width constrained by wall-jump gain, and an upper shelf above the free-jump budget. Other routes use one-way landing modules whose spacing and widths depend on Hero size, speed and jump height. Each room retains typed action records with entry/exit points, landing width and a conservative clearance envelope for inspection and future content rules. Decorative rectangles exclude a conservative swept corridor above each route segment. Smooth walls block movement and dash, but cannot initiate wall slide/jump. Unmarked campaign walls retain their previous semantics.
4. **Two WFC layers.** The constructive main chain supplies required room roles and portal constraints. Room WFC selects combat/traversal variants for branches; local WFC selects compatible landing positions and clear rectangular wall candidates. Both use the shared weighted-entropy solver, propagation and bounded backtracking. There is **no runtime search, repair or reachability filtering of a completed layout**. Incompatible profiles, insufficient space or domain contradictions report a reason and retain the current map.
5. **Tilemaps and collisions.** `WfcDungeonGenerator` pauses the current map and Hero while building a staging generation, one room per frame. Background, ordinary walls, smooth walls and platforms are separate Tilemaps. The explicit nine-tile wall palette selects corners, edges and centres using the final solid occupancy across room boundaries. Ordinary and smooth walls share the same castle sprites and material; smooth walls receive white colouring. Separate Composite colliders preserve ordinary/smooth wall attributes. Platforms use independent thin BoxCollider2D landing strips and a one-way effector; vertically adjacent visual tiles cannot merge into an obstructing side wall. Once every room is ready, the new generation replaces the old one atomically and Hero returns to the protected 3x3 spawn.
6. **Enemies and attacks.** Combat rooms reserve clear floor/flight bays away from portals, with up to two Orcs and one Flying Eye at density 1; unavailable slots reduce the count. Spawn records include patrol boundaries. `GeneratedEnemyBounds` constrains ground movement at edges, flying movement within the room, and acquisition/release by boundary and line of sight. Existing Orc/Flying Eye state machines, animation events, damage and coin rewards remain responsible for combat. Challenge, spawn and exit rooms have no enemy spawns. Only the current and adjacent rooms activate their populations.
7. **Ownership, retry and camera.** `GeneratedMapContent` owns map objects, actors, shots and warnings; replacement destroys the complete previous generation. Retry retains seed, size, density and the immutable movement snapshot. `GameManager` routes death retry back to this generator. The camera uses the same `MapCameraFollow2D` as `stage1_full` and `stage2_full`, with view size 28, smoothing 0.14 and padding 1; runtime generation updates only its target and map bounds. Overview temporarily disables follow and frames the map; returning restores follow and clamps inside the map. The exit only records completion, with no Boss combat or scene transition.

## Single winding room: current WfcDungeon scene

Open `Assets/Scenes/WfcDungeon.unity`, or rebuild with `Tools/World/Build WFC Dungeon`. New maps default to UTC timestamp seeds; disable that option to reproduce a manual seed. Each seed chooses **100–150 interior cells wide and 50–100 high**; explicit widths below 100 normalize to 100. The outer shell adds one cell per side. The Hero starts at the lower left with a protected background-only area; a Boss-door-art trigger at the upper right completes the prototype without starting a Boss fight. This mode has ordinary walls only, double jump and dash unlocked, and existing wall-jump behavior. Kunai hover is outside the movement contract.

### Complete generation and play pipeline

1. **Input and ability snapshot.** After Role and progression initialize, the test Hero unlocks two jumps/dash and `TraversalProfile.Capture` reads effective movement, gravity, capsule dimensions and dash timing. Settings provide seed, optional explicit width/height, density and `routeMultiplier` (default **2.0**). Random dimensions are a deterministic function of seed. The actual fixed physics step is included in the layout's reproduction identifier.
2. **Action budgets.** `WindingTraversalBudget` accounts for fixed-step gravity, an apex second jump, gravity during dash, duration/cooldown and landing time. Only complete dashes count. Compact rising modules use 77–86% of theoretical two-jump height; spacious candidates use 83–89%. Signed descents and horizontal double jumps use their horizontal flight budget. The user-approved sparse design allows compact close-platform fits: **horizontal edge gaps have no universal 75% lower bound**. Editor diagnostics measure final shelf edges separately from centre distance; wider shelves can reduce the actual gap. The first action starts on continuous ground and has no fabricated launch-edge measurement.
3. **Fixed-length route grammar and sparse selection.** Entrance-to-exit Euclidean distance `D` sets target `L = D * routeMultiplier`, in cells. Length sums straight segments through each route landing point to exit. Seeded candidates have 3/5/7/9 alternating legs, elevations, descents and internal horizontal/dash placement. A bounded scalar solve chooses bend amplitude while retaining endpoints and motion budgets, within **0.1 cell** of requested length. Compact and spacious complete candidates participate; only those with the fewest primary landings remain, with spacious candidates weighted more strongly. Spacious primary shelves have at least **3.25 cells** of two-dimensional edge separation; compact fallback has no equivalent global spacing promise. Impossible requests report size, seed and length while retaining the current map.
4. **Route walls and module-level WFC (r5).** Before route collapse, `FitRouteWalls` admits at least one wide top-support wall and one tall side-climb wall with each candidate. Wide walls replace selected horizontal route supports exactly; they are spread along the route, with no extra cap platform. Tall walls occupy the outside of ascending bends, leave the next action on the open side, and reserve continuous contact, outward launch, spare jump, return and exit clearance. The wall model follows Hero's contact jump reset, launch velocity, input lock and air control; incompatible profiles report failure. Shelf positions can shift beside the wall while route endpoints and nominal fixed length stay unchanged. Every solid rectangle avoids other actions and landing headroom. The shared entropy/propagation/backtracking solver then selects a complete admitted route. Optional terrain WFC chooses explicit wide/tall rectangular barriers **without automatic top/side platforms**, retaining `Ceil(nonempty count × 0.75)` spatially spread barriers. Budget exhaustion reports failure. **No runtime completed-map reachability search, repair, or acceptance filtering is performed.**
5. **Tiles and physics.** One room owns the background, ordinary-wall and platform Tilemaps. A nine-part castle palette selects all sides/corners/fill from solid occupancy. Each fractional-position route wall has a local Tilemap and a closed CompositeCollider2D rectangle, using ordinary wall material and climb behavior. A `DungeonLanding.SolidTop` record describes the wide wall's existing support and is excluded from platform sprites and one-way colliders. Other landing positions retain fractional coordinates through per-tile transform matrices and matching thin BoxCollider2D strips. The one-way effector allows upward passage and the existing drop-through controls; platforms never merge into solid stair sides. No smooth-wall renderer/collider is created in this mode.
6. **Settlements and combat.** Encounter planning selects separated sites on existing wide platforms, wall tops, the outer floor, or new rectangular foundations avoiding flight reservations. Target camp count is `Clamp(Round(width × height / 2200), 3, 7)`; density 1 targets four ground enemies (a seeded rotation of Orc, Mushroom and Skeleton) and one eye per camp. Ground-slot spacing/headroom uses the largest participating body; placement and validation use per-species collider sizes and offsets. Insufficient space reduces counts with a warning. Spawn safe radius is 11 cells; camp alerts are capped at 13 cells and 40% of nearest camp distance. `GeneratedEnemyBounds` constrains targets, visibility and movement; supported patrol regions may overlap ordinary route envelopes, while wall-climb corridors remain free of camps. Flying Eyes additionally retain their own `detectionRange`. AI state classes drive Animator parameters; release events create owned projectiles, which retain copied region rules through hit/damage resolution even after owner death. Health and reward guards settle kills once.
7. **Commit, camera and retry.** The generator pauses the Hero and previous content, builds a staging map, then switches after collision and content are ready. A failed request restores the previous generation. `GeneratedMapContent` owns terrain, monsters, projectiles and warnings so replacement clears them together. The stage1/stage2 `MapCameraFollow2D` uses view 28, smoothing 0.14 and padding 1 with the generated bounds. Retry retains actual dimensions, seed, density, route multiplier and movement snapshot; pending panel edits cannot change it. The reproduction ID includes these inputs and generator version. The nominal length describes the designed route: ordinary-wall climbing and advanced player shortcuts are allowed, so it does not promise an enforced shortest path.
